## Study 1 — NCT07406386 (Full Detail)

**Title:** Integrating Halotherapy Into Preseason Training Improves Respiratory and Aerobic Performance in Elite Female Soccer Players
**Sponsor:** Gumushane University, Turkey
**Status:** Completed (Apr 2022 – Jul 2022)
**Design:** Open-label RCT, parallel group
**Participants:** 28 elite female soccer players, ages 18–30, Turkish Women's First League

### Background

Halotherapy (inhalation of dry salt aerosol in a controlled halochamber) has shown pulmonary benefits in clinical populations but was untested in elite athletes. This trial investigated whether adding halotherapy to standard preseason soccer training could yield additional respiratory and aerobic gains.

### Findings

- Both groups completed the same 12-week preseason soccer programme.
- The Halotherapy group additionally received 45-min halochamber sessions, 3x/week for 12 weeks.
- Primary outcomes: MIP, MEP (respiratory muscle strength), VO2max via Yo-Yo IRT Level 1.
- Secondary outcomes: FVC, FEV1, PEF, MVV (spirometry); Yo-Yo total distance.
- No results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov as of Feb 2026.

### Training Protocol

**Arm A — Halotherapy (Experimental)**

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Base programme | Standard 12-week preseason soccer training |
| Add-on | Halotherapy in controlled halochamber |
| Session length | 45 minutes |
| Frequency | 3x/week |
| Duration | 12 weeks |
| Aerosol concentration | ~20 mg/m3 |
| Particle size | less than 5 microns |
| Temperature | 20–24°C |
| Humidity | 40–60% |

**Arm B — Control**

Standard 12-week preseason soccer training only; no add-on.

### Outcome Measures

| Type | Measure | Timeframe |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Primary | MIP — Maximal Inspiratory Pressure | Baseline & 12 wks |
| Primary | MEP — Maximal Expiratory Pressure | Baseline & 12 wks |
| Primary | VO2max (Yo-Yo IRT Level 1) | Baseline & 12 wks |
| Secondary | FVC, FEV1, PEF, MVV (spirometry) | Baseline & 12 wks |
| Secondary | Yo-Yo IRT Level 1 total distance | Baseline & 12 wks |

### Eligibility

- **Inclusion:** Female, 18–30 yrs, Turkish Women's First League, 5+ yrs competitive experience, 10+ hrs/wk training, injury-free, non-smoker, no chronic medication.
- **Exclusion:** Respiratory/cardiovascular/metabolic disease, current injury, smoking, pregnancy.

## Study 2 — NCT04134741 (Full Detail)

**Title:** Neuromuscular Training (Kinetic Control) for Lumbar Spine and Knee Pain and Injury Risk Reduction in Female Football Players
**Sponsor:** John Paul II University, Biala Podlaska, Poland
**Status:** Completed (Jan 2019 – Jun 2019)
**Design:** Single-blind RCT, parallel group (investigator-masked)
**Participants:** 18 female football players, ages 19–25, university club

### Background

Lumbar and knee injuries are highly prevalent in female football. Kinetic Control (KC) is a neuromuscular approach that begins with a functional examination of motor control deficits and then applies individually tailored exercises. This RCT tested whether supplementing standard football training with KC could reduce pain, injury risk, and improve neuromuscular balance and knee torque.

### Findings

- KC group underwent 4 weeks of KC training (3x/week, 20–30 min/session) on top of standard football training.
- The study covered two seasonal cycles (Jul–Dec and Jan–Jun).
- Primary outcomes: pain via VAS, Laitinen's Questionnaire, Stratford's BPFS.
- Secondary outcomes: single-leg knee bend test, GAMMA dynamometric platform (balance), isometric knee flexor/extensor torque (UPR-02A chair).
- Hypothesis: KC training would reduce injury risk and pain while improving static/dynamic balance and knee strength.
- No results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov as of Oct 2019.

### Training Protocol

**Arm A — Kinetic Control Group (Experimental)**

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Base programme | Standard football training |
| Add-on | Kinetic Control (KC) neuromuscular training |
| Session length | 20–30 minutes |
| Frequency | 3x/week |
| Duration | 4 weeks per training cycle |
| Exercise types | Active exercises; active exercises with torso resistance; lower limb exercises |
| Pre-training assessment | Functional examination of motor disorders |
| Supervision | Physiotherapist throughout |

**Arm B — Traditional Training Group (Control)**

Standard football training only; no add-on.

### Outcome Measures

| Type | Measure | Timeframe |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Primary | VAS Pain Scale (0–10) | Baseline & 6 months |
| Primary | Modified Laitinen's Pain Questionnaire (0–16) | Baseline & 6 months |
| Primary | Stratford's Back Pain Functional Scale (0–60) | Baseline & 6 months |
| Secondary | Single Leg Small Knee Bend Test (0–2) | Baseline & 6 months |
| Secondary | GAMMA Dynamometric Platform (balance) | Baseline & 6 months |
| Secondary | Isometric Knee Torque — UPR-02A chair | Baseline & 6 months |

### Eligibility

- **Inclusion:** Female football players; no NSAIDs 1+ week before start; written consent.
- **Exclusion:** Acute neuralgia/disc herniation needing surgery; lumbar/knee injury within 6 months; acute knee inflammation; post-knee surgery.

## Brief Notes — Studies 3–5

**Study 3 — NCT06800079 (Recruiting, Portugal)**
Elastic band resistance training, 8 weeks, 2x/week, 20 min/session, 6 exercises across 2 sessions. Across U15, U19, and Senior female players. Outcomes: isometric knee strength, vertical jump (CMJ), change-of-direction (Modified T-Test).

**Study 4 — NCT06445478 (Completed, Turkey)**
BFR-RE (4 sets: 30/15/15/15 reps, 20–40% 1RM, 30–60s rest, 2x/week, 8 weeks) vs. ID-RE (leg press, squat, knee extension, 2x/week, 8 weeks) in 30 female players. Outcomes: isokinetic strength, limb circumference, knee ROM, Illinois Agility Test, WHOQOL-27.

**Study 5 — NCT06774976 (Not Yet Recruiting, Sweden)**
Neck resistance training, 8 weeks, 2–3x/week, static + dynamic cervical exercises with head harness and resistance tubing. Aim: reduce head accelerations and improve isometric neck strength to lower sport-related concussion risk.

## Key Themes

1. **Short-to-medium protocols** — 4 to 12 weeks; 8 weeks is most common.
2. **Low frequency, high specificity** — 2–3 supplementary sessions/week added on top of normal football training.
3. **Injury prevention dominates** — neuromuscular control, balance, and pain reduction feature in 3 of 5 studies.
4. **Non-traditional modalities** — halotherapy, BFR, elastic bands alongside classical resistance training.
5. **Small sample sizes** — 18–60 participants; a consistent limitation across all trials.
6. **No published results** — none of the 5 trials had posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov at time of extraction.
