## Intervention Protocol

### Group A — Kinetic Control (KC) Neuromuscular Training + Standard Training

> **Kinetic Control** is a movement-control assessment and rehabilitation framework developed by Mark Comerford and Sarah Mottram (Kinetic Control Ltd, UK). It focuses on identifying and correcting **movement system impairments** — particularly uncontrolled movement at specific joints — through targeted active retraining.

**Programme Structure:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Frequency | 3 sessions/week |
| Duration | 4 weeks per season (×2 seasons = 8 weeks total) |
| Session length | 20–30 minutes |
| Supervision | Physiotherapist-assisted |
| Setting | In addition to regular football training |

**KC Training Content:**
1. **Functional movement assessment** — identification of individual motor disorders
2. **Active exercises** — targeted movement retraining
3. **Active exercises with trunk/torso muscle resistance** — core stabilisation under load
4. **Lower limb exercises** — hip/knee/ankle neuromuscular control

**Assessment Schedule:**
- Pre-season (start of each 6-month cycle)
- Post-season (end of each 6-month cycle)

### Group B — Standard Training Only
- Regular football training programme
- No additional physiotherapy or neuromuscular training

## Outcome Measures

### Primary Outcomes (Pain & Function)

| Measure | Scale | Protocol | Timeframe |
|---------|-------|----------|-----------|
| Pain intensity — VAS | 0–10 (10 = worst pain) | Self-reported | Start and end of each season |
| Pain — Modified Laitinen Questionnaire | 0–16 (16 = worst) | 4 indicators: intensity, frequency, analgesic use, motor limitation | Start and end of each season |
| Back function — Stratford BPFS | 0–60 (60 = best function) | 12 everyday activities rated 0–5 | Start and end of each season |

### Secondary Outcomes (Neuromuscular Function)

| Measure | Protocol | Timeframe |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Single leg small knee bend (SLSKB) test | Standing; transfer weight to one leg; slight knee bend; photographed; axis analysis by software. Score: 2 (correct 1st attempt), 1 (correct after guidance), 0 (incorrect twice) | Start and end of each season |
| Dynamometric platform tests (GAMMA) | 4 tests: (1) standing eyes open 30s, (2) standing eyes closed 30s, (3) squat test (3 phases), (4) jump test (3 phases). Proper load ratio = 1.0–1.15 between limbs | Start and end of each season |
| Muscle force torque — UPR-02A chair | Isometric; 30° knee flexion (flexors), 60° knee flexion (extensors); 2 measurements per muscle group per limb; sequence: R extension → R flexion → L extension → L flexion | Start and end of each season |

## Kinetic Control Framework — Key Principles

```
KINETIC CONTROL ASSESSMENT:
1. Identify the level of uncontrolled movement (UCM)
2. Determine the direction of UCM (flexion, extension, rotation, lateral shift)
3. Classify: local stabiliser deficit vs. global stabiliser deficit
4. Prescribe: low-load activation → range training → functional integration

TRAINING PROGRESSION:
Phase 1: Dissociation training (isolate movement at target segment)
Phase 2: Loading progressions (add resistance once control is achieved)
Phase 3: Functional integration (sport-specific movement patterns)
```

## Key Research Questions Addressed

1. Does KC neuromuscular training reduce lumbar spine and knee injury risk in female footballers?
2. Does it reduce pain intensity and frequency?
3. Does it improve knee flexor/extensor torque and stability?
4. Does it reduce disability and improve functional level?

---

## Clinical Significance

This study is significant because it addresses the **dual burden** of female football athletes:
- High prevalence of **lumbar spine pain** (often overlooked vs. knee injuries)
- ACL and knee injury risk (well-documented)

The KC framework is particularly valuable because it:
- Is **individualised** to each player's specific movement deficits
- Requires **minimal equipment** (bodyweight and resistance bands)
- Is **time-efficient** (20–30 min sessions)
- Can be integrated into warm-up or cool-down routines

## Eligibility Criteria

**Inclusion:**
- Female football player
- No NSAIDs for ≥1 week before study start
- Written informed consent

**Exclusion:**
- Acute neuralgia or massive disc herniation requiring surgery
- Lumbar spine or knee injury within 6 months prior
- Acute knee inflammation
- Post-knee surgery

---

## Practical Takeaways for Coaches

1. **Kinetic Control screening** should be part of pre-season assessments — identify UCM before it becomes injury
2. **3 sessions/week of 20–30 min** NMT is feasible within a football training schedule
3. **SLSKB test** is a quick, equipment-free screening tool for knee neuromuscular control
4. **Dynamometric platform testing** provides objective bilateral load symmetry data
5. **Lumbar spine health** deserves equal attention to knee health in female football injury prevention programmes
