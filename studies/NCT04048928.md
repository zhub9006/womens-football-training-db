## Intervention Protocol

### Training Group (TG) — Maximal Strength Training (MST)

> **Core exercise:** Free-barbell back squat at ~90° knee angle

**Programme Structure:**

| Phase | Sessions | Sets | Reps | Intensity | Rest |
|-------|----------|------|------|-----------|------|
| Weeks 1–1.5 (3 sessions) | 3 | 3 | 6 | ≥85% 1RM | 180 sec between sets |
| Weeks 2–5 (7 sessions) | 7 | 4 | 4 | ≥85% 1RM (progressive overload) | 180 sec between sets |

**Progressive Overload Rule:**
- Load started at **85% of pre-test 1RM**
- If athlete completed more than 6 reps (phase 1) or 4 reps (phase 2), load was increased by **2.5–10 kg**

**Ancillary Exercise (Injury Prevention):**
- **Nordic hamstring curl:** 3 sets × 6 reps after every squat session
- Rest: ≥180 sec between sets
- Rationale: prevent hamstring strain from agonist-antagonist strength ratio imbalance

**Frequency:** 2 sessions/week × 5 weeks = 10 total sessions  
**Additional training:** Regular pre-season team training continued alongside MST

**Warm-Up Protocol (each session):**
1. 7 min self-selected low-intensity cycling (ergometer)
2. 7 min low-intensity running on artificial grass

### Control Group (CG)
- Regular planned pre-season training only
- No additional strength training

## Outcome Measures

### Primary Outcome
| Measure | Protocol | Timeframe |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Sprint time (5m, 10m, 15m) | Photocells (ATU-X, IC Control AB); 3 trials, best recorded; 180 sec rest between trials; standing start 30 cm behind line | Pre → Post (5 weeks) |

### Secondary Outcomes
| Measure | Protocol | Timeframe |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Countermovement jump (CMJ) height | Portable force platform (1200 Hz); hands on hips; feet shoulder-width; 2 trials, best recorded | Pre → Post (5 weeks) |
| 1RM back squat | Free-barbell squat at ~90° knee angle; 5 attempts average; increments of 5–10 kg | Pre → Post (5 weeks) |

## Key Findings

> ⚠️ *Study completed; results published (PMID 31534773). Summary below:*

- **1RM squat improved significantly** in the training group
- **Sprint times and CMJ height did NOT significantly improve** in the training group vs. control
- **Conclusion:** A 5-week MST block, while effective at increasing maximal strength, is **insufficient in isolation** to transfer to sprint/jump performance in female footballers — likely requiring longer programmes or concurrent plyometric training

---

## Practical Takeaways for Coaches

1. **5 weeks is likely too short** for strength-to-power transfer — plan at least 8–12 weeks
2. **Nordic hamstring curls are a non-negotiable add-on** when programming heavy squats
3. **Progressive overload works** even in short blocks — load should be adjusted session-by-session
4. **Cluster randomisation by team** is the appropriate design when individual randomisation risks contamination

## Eligibility Criteria

**Inclusion:**
- Female football player on one of the two participating teams
- Self-reported injury-free

**Exclusion:**
- Any injury preventing strength training, running, or jumping
