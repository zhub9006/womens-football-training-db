## Intervention Protocol

### Group 1 — Blood Flow Restriction Resistance Exercise (BFR-RE)

> **Mechanism:** A pneumatic cuff is placed at the proximal thigh to partially occlude venous outflow during low-load resistance exercise, creating a metabolic environment similar to high-load training.

**Exercise Parameters:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Frequency | 2 sessions/week |
| Duration | 8 weeks |
| Sets per exercise | 4 sets |
| Reps (Set 1) | 30 repetitions |
| Reps (Sets 2–4) | 15 repetitions each |
| Total reps per exercise | 75 repetitions |
| Rest between sets | 30–60 seconds |
| Load | 20–40% of 1 Repetition Maximum (1RM) |
| Cuff placement | Proximal lower extremity (thigh) |
| Target muscles | Lower extremity muscle groups |

**Assessments at:** Baseline, Week 4, Week 8

---

### Group 2 — Isokinetic Device Resistance Exercise (ID-RE)

> **Mechanism:** Resistance exercise performed on an isokinetic device (Kineo Intelligent Load), which controls movement velocity and provides variable resistance throughout the range of motion.

**Exercise Programme:**

| Exercise | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Leg press | Progressive resistance / position changes based on athlete condition |
| Squat | Progressive resistance |
| Knee extension | Progressive resistance |

**Parameters:**
- Frequency: 2 sessions/week
- Duration: 8 weeks
- Progression: Resistance increased or position modified based on athlete status

## Illinois Agility Test Protocol

```
Track Setup:
- Length: 10 m
- Width: 5 m
- 3 intermediate cones at 3.3 m intervals
- 40 m total running distance (flat)
- 20 m between cones (slalom)

Start Position:
- Prone, hands at shoulder level on ground
- On signal: sprint, 180° turns every 10m, slalom section

Scoring: Best of 2 trials (seconds)
```

## Key Research Questions Addressed

1. Is BFR-RE as effective as isokinetic device training for improving muscle strength?
2. Does BFR-RE produce hypertrophy (circumference gains) comparable to conventional resistance training?
3. Which modality produces greater improvements in agility?
4. Are there quality-of-life differences between modalities?

---

## Clinical Significance

This is one of the **first RCTs applying BFR training in female football players**, a modality previously studied mainly in:
- Post-surgical rehabilitation
- Older adults with sarcopenia
- Male athletes

The low-load nature of BFR (20–40% 1RM) makes it particularly relevant for:
- In-season training (reduced mechanical load)
- Return-to-play protocols after lower limb injury
- Players with pain or load tolerance restrictions

## Eligibility Criteria

**Inclusion:**
- Female football player, age 18–30
- Voluntary participation

**Exclusion:**
- Deep vein thrombosis
- Pregnancy
- Hypertension
- Anti-inflammatory drug use
- Unexplained chest pain
- Congenital heart disease
- Any contraindication to exercise
- Concurrent participation in another trial

---

## Practical Takeaways for Coaches

1. **BFR training at 20–40% 1RM** can produce strength gains comparable to conventional loading — valuable for in-season maintenance
2. **75 total reps per exercise** (30+15+15+15) is the standard BFR set-rep scheme
3. **30–60 sec rest** between sets is shorter than conventional S&C — important for session efficiency
4. **Illinois Agility Test** is a validated and easy-to-administer agility assessment for this population
5. **Isokinetic testing** provides the most precise bilateral strength data but requires specialist equipment
